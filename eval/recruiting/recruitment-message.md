# Recruitment messages

Adapt freely. The one thing worth keeping is the **reciprocity** — it converts the ask from a
favour into a trade, which is why people say yes.

---

## Group chat / cohort channel

> **Free 15-min mock technical interview — and you get feedback**
>
> I'm building an AI interviewer for a personal project and I need real interview sessions to
> test it against. Looking for ~10 people.
>
> **What it is:** 15 minutes, you answer 3–5 spoken technical questions (system design,
> debugging, general SWE). Your voice is recorded and transcribed.
>
> **What you get:** written feedback on your answers, and a second mock run whenever you want
> one before your real interviews. Fall recruiting is starting and practising out loud is the
> part everyone skips.
>
> **What I get:** a transcript to test my grading model against.
>
> Audio stays on my machine and never goes in the repo. Transcripts get anonymised and may end
> up in a public GitHub repo — I'll show you yours before anything is committed, and you can
> pull out at any point.
>
> Reply if you're in and I'll send a time.

## Direct message

> Hey — I'm building an AI interviewer and need ~10 people for 15-minute mock technical
> interviews to test the grading against. You'd answer a few spoken SWE questions; I'd record
> and transcribe it.
>
> In exchange I'll give you written feedback and run a practice interview for you whenever you
> want before real interviews start.
>
> Audio never leaves my laptop; transcripts get anonymised and might go in a public repo, and
> you'd see yours first. Interested?

---

## The second-labeler ask — say this at the end of a session, not by message

Timing matters more than wording. Ask while they are still in the room and still interested,
not in a follow-up message three days later.

> One more thing, and it's a smaller ask than what you just did.
>
> To know whether my grader is any good, I need to know how much two *humans* disagree with
> each other on the same transcript — otherwise its score has nothing to compare against.
>
> Would you rate 10 transcripts? Reading and scoring on four dimensions, about an hour, and you
> can do it whenever. I'll send a guide.

Only one person across all ten sessions has to say yes.

---

## Notes

- **Do not oversell it as "an AI interview".** Sessions run on the existing scripted flow. What
  is being collected is honest answers to real questions, not a demo.
- **Do not promise a score.** Feedback is qualitative. Promising a number invites arguing with
  the number.
- **Be direct that transcripts may go public.** Burying it costs more later than it saves now,
  and someone will read the repo.
